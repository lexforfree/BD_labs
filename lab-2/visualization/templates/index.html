Bayesian Smoothing — Beta(2,2) Prior

Why Bayes?

The empirical estimate wins / total is unreliable for rare situations (e.g. 4th down while losing by 14 in the 1st quarter — only a handful of plays ever reached that state). A Bayesian approach adds a prior belief and updates it with observed data.

Formula:

P(win | data) = (wins + α) / (total + α + β)

With α = β = 2: prior mean = 0.5, equivalent strength = 4 pseudo-observations.

Effect on estimates:

  • Dense buckets (total > 500): MLE ≈ Bayes — prior has negligible effect
  • Sparse buckets (total = 10–30): Bayes pulls estimate toward 0.5, reducing extreme values caused by noise
  • The larger the prior strength (α + β), the stronger the regularisation

Connection to Beta distribution:

Posterior is Beta(α + wins, β + losses). The posterior mean is exactly the formula above. Beta(1,1) = uniform (no prior knowledge); Beta(2,2) = mild belief that 50/50 is a reasonable default.
